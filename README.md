## Build Process

### -Mobo prep

I learnt that the CPU only fits in one way because of the socket design.

I learnt that to install the ram you need to apply a lot more pressure than you might think.

I learnt that M.2 SSDs should be installed at an angle before being pushed down and secured with a screw. This keeps the drive firmly connected and prevents movement.

I learnt that you should always apply a pea sized amount of thermal paste to a CPU because to little can cause overheating while too much thermal paste can create a mess and can reduce the effectiveness of the cooler mounting pressure.

I learnt that when you are installing the CPU cooler that you need to remove the installation sticker otherwise no heat will be dissipated.

I also learnt that you should always screw down the CPU cooler all the way otherwise it may not make full contact with the CPU and prevent heat from dissipating.

### -Mobo installation

I learnt that the Motherboard cannot make full contact with the case because of the exposed pins and traces on the back of it  which could cause a short, destroying the Motherboard. The standoffs keep the motherboard raised above the Case and prevent electrical shorts.

I also learnt that not all of the Motherboard standoff screw holes need to be used since the case I used did not have all the standoffs my Motherboard had.

I also learnt that installing the I/O shield is a pain. But, it is necessary to ground and protect the ports

### -Final steps

I learnt that cable management is definately worth the time and effort.

I learnt that not all cases have an RGB/fan hub.

I learnt that prewiring is very useful.

I learnt that GPU sag brackets do not need to be expensive, and temporary solutions can work. My bracket was made from LEGO.

I learnt that the PC will take extra long to POST the first time you turn it on. This happens because the motherboard performs hardware checks and memory training.

## Challenges

### Here are some challenges I faced during my first PC build:

-I was experiencing lots of trouble on how to screw my Motherboard to the standoffs on the case. But I realised I was using the wrong screws after reading the Case manual and managed to screw down the Motherboard correctly.

-I initially installed the PSU in the wrong orientation, causing it to draw warmer air from inside the case instead of cooler air from outside the case. So I unplugged all of the cables and reseated the PSU to let it intake cool air from under the Case.

-I connected my CPU cooler incorrectly, causing the fan and ARGB lighting to not work correctly. I fixed this by connecting the fan cable to the CPU_FAN header and the ARGB cable to the correct RGB header.

-The Case fan RGB lighting was not working because I had forgotten to connect the SATA power cable from the PSU to the RGB/fan hub.

## What I Would Do Differently Next Time

### After completing my first PC build, there are several things I would improve for future builds:

-Read the case and motherboard manuals before starting the build. This would make it easier to identify screws, connectors, and installation steps.

-Prewire cables before installing components where possible. This would make cable management easier and reduce the chance of forgetting connections.

-Choose a better case. My current case worked, but a higher quality case would improve cable management, airflow, and the overall building experience.

-Prepare a Windows installation USB before building. This would make the first boot and setup process faster.

-Complete cable management during the build instead of leaving it until later. Doing it while installing components would make the final result cleaner and reduce the need to redo cables.
